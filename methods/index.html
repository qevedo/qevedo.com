Methods

The techniques behind each tool: where each comes from, what it does on our benchmarks, and where its code is. Some reproduce published methods, some extend them, and some were designed here. The measured effects come from the scripts behind the benchmarks.

Compiler

Phase folding published

Every wire carries a parity of the circuit's inputs: CNOTs XOR parities, X gates flip them, and other gates start new ones. Diagonal rotations on the same parity merge into one, however many gates lie between them, and the CNOTs they kept apart then cancel.

Adders: 19% fewer CNOTs than Qiskit at level 3 (196 against 241 at 32 qubits); QFT, QAOA and Heisenberg chains: 7–25% fewer gates in all; compiling through CNOTs first does the same for CZ devices (adders: 40 CZs instead of 49).

Amy, Maslov and Mosca, IEEE TCAD 2014; Nam, Ross, Su, Childs and Maslov, npj QI 2018 · phase_folding.rs

Rotation merging through Cliffords published

Phase folding stops at Hadamards. Moved through every Clifford gate before it with a stabilizer tableau, each Z rotation is a rotation about a Pauli string, and rotations about the same string merge whenever everything between them commutes with it.

Zhang and Chen, arXiv:1903.12456 · pauli_network.rs

Pauli networks extended

A Hamiltonian simulation step is a list of Pauli rotations, each conventionally written with its own CNOT ladders. QCC reads the circuit back as rotations and one Clifford, and builds it again greedily: each controlled-Pauli gate is the one that most lowers the weights of the rotations coming next, so commuting rotations share their CNOTs. It is kept only when it needs no more two-qubit gates and no more gates in all.

One Trotter step of random Hamiltonians: 44% fewer CNOTs than Qiskit at 8 qubits (107 against 191) and 20% at 16 (313 against 393), with 38% and 19% fewer gates in all.

After Rustiq: Goubault de Brugière and Martiel, arXiv:2404.03280, and pytket's GreedyPauliSimp, whose seeded trials QCC also runs; Clifford synthesis after Bravyi et al., arXiv:2105.02291 · pauli_network.rs

Cheap frames for Pauli networks ours

Single-qubit gates are most of what a Pauli network costs. The basis changes that set up each CNOT are not undone after it: the rotations still to come are read in the new frame, which leaves their weights alone, and the frame is paid for once, at the end. Each CNOT is placed whichever way round needs fewer basis changes, and those use √X, native on IBM devices, rather than Hadamards.

About half the network's single-qubit gates: on a 16-qubit Hamiltonian, 792 gates in all instead of 1285, against Qiskit's 972 and pytket's 887.

pauli_network.rs

Two-qubit synthesis in context ours

Runs of gates on one pair of qubits are resynthesized from their 4×4 unitary with the fewest two-qubit gates (KAK). Many syntheses use that many; they differ in their single-qubit gates. A block's outer gates merge with its neighbours' afterwards, so QCC picks the one with the fewest gates between its first and last two-qubit gate, not the fewest in isolation.

Quantum Volume: 1% fewer gates than synthesizing each block alone, which brings QCC level with Qiskit there.

KAK and templates after Vatan and Williams, PRA 2004 · two_qubit.rs

Moving gates through symmetric blocks ours

An XX + YY + ZZ interaction, as in Heisenberg models, commutes with the same rotation on both qubits, so the single-qubit gates around such a block are not fixed: a factor can pass through it from one side to the other. Blocks are synthesized one at a time, so QCC then picks, for each symmetric block, the factor that leaves the gates between it and its neighbours cheapest.

Heisenberg chains: 8–9% fewer gates with CX (1173 against Qiskit's 1568 at 32 qubits), and with CZ 7–13% fewer than Qiskit instead of a fifth more.

symmetric_blocks.rs

Gathering blocks through commuting gates extended

Two gates on the same pair of qubits fall in different blocks when a gate on one of them and a third qubit lies between, even if that gate commutes with one of them: cx a,b; cx a,c; cx b,a is a SWAP and one CNOT once cx a,c moves aside. QCC moves such gates out of the way when they commute exactly with what they cross (single-qubit gates counted together), taking along the gates that depend on them, and only where the gathered block needs fewer CNOTs; the circuit is kept only when it needs no more two-qubit gates and no more gates in all.

Random Clifford+T: 150 CNOTs instead of 154 at 16 qubits, and 310 at 32, as many as pytket with fewer gates in all (1034 against 1039). On a heavy-hex chip: the 16-qubit QFT 353 CNOTs instead of 357 (Qiskit 356), the 16-qubit QAOA 180 instead of 188, both with CX; four of the 32 chip results got worse in one count or both.

As tket's Clifford simplification: Fagan and Duncan, arXiv:1901.10114; Sivarajah et al., arXiv:2003.10611 · commuting_blocks.rs

Single-qubit trades through CNOTs ours

A CNOT commutes with a Z rotation on its control and an X rotation on its target, so the runs of single-qubit gates on either side can hand such a rotation across. QCC chooses the angles that make both runs cheapest. It hides Z rotations from phase folding, so it runs only on a circuit that will not be optimized again.

With gathering, random Clifford+T: 278, 505 and 1034 gates in all at 8, 16 and 32 qubits, instead of 293, 525 and 1074.

symmetric_blocks.rs

SWAP elision and permutation-aware synthesis extended

A SWAP costs three CNOTs; relabelling the qubits after it costs nothing. The same holds for a SWAP hidden in a block: a CNOT each way is a SWAP and one CNOT, so a block that is a SWAP away from fewer CNOTs is synthesized that way. QCC relabels whenever the moved qubits are measured last, so the output is exactly equivalent, and otherwise when asked to, reporting where each qubit's state ends.

QFT: 12, 24 and 48 fewer CNOTs at 8, 16 and 32 qubits; random Clifford+T: up to 5% fewer.

As Qiskit's ElidePermutations and pytket's implicit wire swaps · permutation.rs

Numerical synthesis for any entangling gate ours

For native gates without a known template (iSWAP, √iSWAP, √SWAP, …), the fewest uses are found numerically: first matching the target's Makhlin invariants, then the single-qubit gates around them, both by Levenberg–Marquardt from seeded starts.

Try it in the decomposition playground · numeric.rs

Layout and routing

Perfect layouts extended

When the pairs of qubits a circuit's gates use form a graph that fits inside the chip's coupling graph, a subgraph search places them so that no SWAP is needed. The circuit is optimized first, so fewer pairs remain to fit.

GHZ states and Heisenberg chains on a heavy-hex chip: no SWAP at all.

As Qiskit's VF2Layout · perfect.rs

SWAPs counted for what they cost published

A SWAP right after gates on the same pair merges into them: after a CNOT it costs one more CNOT, after a generic block nothing. The router lowers the score of such SWAPs by the CNOTs they save, and keeps the routing whose SWAPs cost least once merged.

With more layout trials, 8% fewer two-qubit gates on a heavy-hex chip: the 16-qubit QFT went from 479 CNOTs to 357, and QCC alone used 5% fewer than Qiskit's level 3 (6.5% with CX since 0.4.2).

NASSC: Liu, Li and Zhou, HPCA 2022; SABRE: Li, Ding and Xie, ASPLOS 2019 · sabre.rs

Multi-core mapping

Hungarian Qubit Assignment published

Assigns qubits to the cores of a modular machine slice by slice, solving each slice as an assignment problem with lookahead, so that the qubits of each gate meet in one core with few teleportations.

1.24× fewer teleportations than FGP-rOEE over 105 configurations, 7.8× faster.

Escofet et al., arXiv:2309.12182 · multicore/

Latency-aware assignment ours

Counting teleportations treats every move alike, but a move that waits for a busy link delays its gate. HQA-latency also weighs how long each move would hold up its gate, given how many EPR pairs each link can supply at once.

6–12% shorter circuits than HQA on average, and never longer.

Routing with communication ports ours

Inside each core, qubits are routed with SABRE to fixed communication qubits wired to the links, with an exchange when a port is taken, and EPR pairs are scheduled into the links' free time.

On TeleSABRE's own device and circuits: 0.26× its EPR pairs and 0.51× its SWAPs, and no deadlocks.

SABRE: Li, Ding and Xie, ASPLOS 2019 · routing/

Simulator

Fused, cache-blocked state vectors extended

The state is stored as qsim stores it, eight real and eight imaginary parts per cache line, with AVX2 kernels for 1–6-qubit gates. Dependent gates fuse into matrices of up to 4 qubits, and every gate of a stage runs on 1 MiB regions that stay in L2, relabelling qubits instead of moving them back.

830 gates become 69 kernels and 9 sweeps over memory; 3–4× faster than qsim.

qevedo-simulator

Stabilizer tableaux and Pauli frames extended

Clifford circuits run once on a tableau that switches between row and column layouts as the work requires; the other shots propagate bit-packed Pauli frames in parallel, cache-sized blocks.

A distance-101 surface code, 20,401 qubits: 10,000 shots in 1.5 s, 8× faster than Stim.

After Aaronson and Gottesman, PRA 2004, and Gidney, Quantum 2021

Pauli noise in the frames published

A Pauli error only multiplies a shot's Pauli frame, so noisy Clifford circuits keep one noiseless reference shot and every shot adds its own errors. The sampler draws the geometric gap to the next error rather than a random bit per qubit and shot. Channels wait until a gate touches their qubits, so a layer's noise is one step.

A distance-101 surface code with circuit-level noise, 10,000 shots: 6.0 s on 4 vCPUs against Stim's 37.5 s (14.9 s on one thread), with the same detection statistics.

Gidney, Quantum 2021 · qevedo-simulator

Detection events from Stim circuits extended

Stim's circuit format, read with its detector and observable annotations, repeat blocks and measurement-flip probabilities. A detector fires when its measurements' parity differs from the noiseless reference shot's, as in Stim; measurement flips are drawn after the run, since they do not touch the state. The shot-major output decoders take is written in Rust, in parallel, by walking only the set bits, straight into the array NumPy sees.

Stim's generated distance-51 surface code, 10,000 shots: 1.63 s on 4 vCPUs against 9.92 s for Stim's detector sampler (2.32 s on one thread), with the same detection rates and logical error rates.

Gidney, Quantum 2021 · qevedo-simulator

Sparse Pauli propagation extended

For an expectation value, the observable is propagated backwards through the circuit as a sum of Pauli strings: Clifford gates collect in a frame and cost nothing, each non-Clifford rotation can split a string in two, and strings with small coefficients are dropped. The strings live in 64 hash maps that each rotation updates in two parallel passes.

<Z62> of IBM's 127-qubit kicked Ising utility circuit: the same value and strings as IBM's pauli-prop, 15–117× faster end to end on 4 threads.

Sparse Pauli dynamics after Begušić, Gray and Chan, arXiv:2308.05077 · qevedo-simulator

Clifford frames around a small dense state ours

For Clifford circuits with a few T gates, the state is a Clifford frame applied to a dense state of only the qubits the non-Clifford gates touch. The peak size is computed before simulating, so the backend can be chosen automatically.

200 qubits with 20 T gates: 1000 shots in 0.11 s.
