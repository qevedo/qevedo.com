Multi-core mapping playground

A modular quantum computer joins small processors, cores, with quantum links; a two-qubit gate needs both qubits in one core, and moving a qubit between cores costs a teleportation. Pick a machine and a circuit, and compare how many teleportations each mapper needs. It runs in your browser: the QCC core, compiled from Rust to WebAssembly.

HQA is the Hungarian Qubit Assignment of Escofet, Ovide, Almudéver, Alarcón and Abadal (arXiv:2309.12182, ACM TQC 2025): before each step of the circuit, it assigns the gates whose qubits are apart to cores with the Hungarian algorithm, drawn to where the qubits are needed next. HQA-latency also weighs how much each move delays its gate. FGP-rOEE (Baker et al., 2020) repartitions the qubits before every step; the naive mapper moves one qubit of each split gate.

Teleportations count one per link crossed. The duration is in two-qubit gate times: gates take 1, each link makes one EPR pair at a time, in 10, and a teleportation takes 5 per link once its pair is ready. Routing inside each core, measured on real circuits, and much more are in the documentation.
